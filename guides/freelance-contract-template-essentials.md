# Freelance Contract Template: The 10 Clauses Every Freelancer Needs (Plain English)

*You don't need a scary legal document. You need ten clear clauses. Here's what they are and why each one exists.*

> **Not legal advice.** This is a practical starting point in plain English. Contract law varies by country and state — for engagements above a few thousand dollars, have a local attorney review your master agreement once, then reuse it forever.

## The 10 clauses

**1. Services.** What you'll deliver, referencing an attached Scope of Work. If it isn't in the scope, it isn't in the price.

**2. Payment.** Amount, schedule (e.g., 50% on signing, 50% on delivery), due dates (net 14), and late fees (e.g., 1.5%/month).

**3. Revisions.** How many rounds are included (2 is standard) and what extra rounds cost. This one clause kills most scope creep.

**4. Timeline dependency.** Your dates depend on the client delivering feedback and materials within X business days; their delays shift all deadlines accordingly.

**5. Ownership.** Transfers to the client **only upon full payment**. You keep the right to show the work in your portfolio unless they object in writing.

**6. Confidentiality.** Both parties protect non-public information shared during the project.

**7. Cancellation / kill fee.** Either side can end it with written notice; the client pays for work completed plus a kill fee (25% of remaining value is common) to cover time you reserved.

**8. Independent contractor.** You're not an employee — your own taxes, insurance, and equipment.

**9. Liability cap.** Your total liability is limited to the fees paid; no liability for indirect damages.

**10. Disputes + governing law.** Informal resolution first, then mediation; the laws of [your state/country] govern.

## The two clauses freelancers skip (and regret)

- **Work pause:** "If any invoice is more than 14 days overdue, all work pauses until the account is current, and all deadlines shift accordingly." This is your only real leverage mid-project.
- **Acceptance by silence:** "Deliverables are accepted when approved in writing, or automatically 7 days after delivery if no feedback is given." This ends the infinite-limbo problem.

## The Scope of Work: the contract's most important attachment

Half the protection isn't in the contract — it's in the SOW: in-scope list (specific, countable), out-of-scope list (kill the common assumptions), milestones with dates, client responsibilities, and the acceptance rule above. When a client says "I thought that was included," the SOW is the answer.
