# How Much Should I Charge? A Freelancer's Pricing Guide

The most-googled freelance question has a terrible official answer ("it depends") and a practical one. Here's the practical one.

## Step 1 — Find your floor (survival rate)

```
Monthly personal expenses          $______
+ Monthly business expenses        $______
+ Taxes (≈25–30% of the above)     $______
= Monthly revenue floor            $______
÷ Billable hours per month (≈80)   ÷ 80
= Hourly floor                     $______
```

Two honest adjustments:
- **Billable ≠ working.** Of 160 working hours, at most half are billable. The rest is admin, marketing, invoicing, and waiting for clients to reply. Use 80, not 160.
- **Taxes are not optional.** Set aside 25–30% before you feel the money.

Any rate below your floor is you paying the client to work for them.

## Step 2 — Find the market band

Your floor tells you the minimum; the market tells you the realistic range. Triangulate from:

- Public rate pages and portfolios of freelancers one level above you
- Job posts that list budgets (Upwork, Contra, We Work Remotely)
- Two direct conversations: ask freelancers in your niche what they charge. Most will tell you if you ask privately and share yours first.

Place yourself in the band by proof: portfolio strength, niche specialization, speed, and testimonials. Specialist beats generalist at every band — "SaaS onboarding emails" outprices "copywriter" by 2–3×.

## Step 3 — Quote the project, not the hour

Hourly billing punishes you for getting faster. Once you know your effective hourly target, translate it into project prices:

```
Estimated hours × 1.4 (things always take 40% longer) × target rate
```

Then present **three tiers**. Clients anchor on the middle option, and a meaningful minority pick the top one:

| Tier | Contains | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Essential | The deliverable, 1 revision round | 1.0× |
| Standard | Deliverable + extras they'd ask for anyway, 2 rounds | 1.6× |
| Premium | Everything + speed/strategy/priority | 2.5× |

## Step 4 — Raise prices on a schedule

Raise rates **every 6 months or every 3 booked clients, whichever comes first**, by 10–20%. Signals you're underpriced:

- Nobody has pushed back on a quote in 6 months
- You're fully booked 2+ weeks out
- Clients say yes in under an hour

Existing clients get 30 days' notice and the old rate until then — the exact email is in the kit (see below).

## The psychological rule

Price is a story about confidence told in numbers. A freelancer charging $95/hr with a clear scope and a calm tone wins work over one charging $50/hr apologetically. State the number. Then stop talking.
