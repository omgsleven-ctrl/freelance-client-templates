# The Scope-Creep Script

**When to use:** a client asks for extra work outside the agreed scope — "can you also just…"

**The principle:** never say no. Say "yes, and here's what it costs." The request becomes a business decision, not a favor.

## The script

```
Hi [First name],

Happy to add [requested item] — it's a good idea.

Since it's outside our agreed scope (item [X] in the Scope of
Work), I'll treat it as a change order: it adds [X hours / $X]
and moves the delivery date to [new date].

If you'd like to go ahead, just confirm and I'll send a one-line
amendment to sign. Or if you'd rather keep the current budget
and timeline, we can park it as a "phase two" item after launch.

Either way works for me — your call.

[Your name]
```

## Why it works

You're not the villain — the scope document is. The client can have anything they want; they just can't have it free. Most clients either pay or happily defer. Either way, you keep the relationship and the boundary.

## No written scope yet?

Retro-document it first: "To make sure we're aligned, here's my understanding of what's included in the project: [list]. Flag anything I've missed by [day]." Then the script above has something to point at.
